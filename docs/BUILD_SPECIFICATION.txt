## 6. Rent, cheques and financial correctness

Implement:
- Rent schedules and individual dues.
- Manual bank transfer and other payment records.
- Partial payments.
- Payment allocations.
- Post-dated cheque records.
- Arrears, aging and follow-up history.
- Invoices, receipts and tenant statements.
- Security-deposit receipts, deductions and refunds.
- Configurable tax policies.
- Recorded property expenses and financial exports.

Cheques must include number, bank, date, amount, related lease and status:
- Scheduled.
- Deposited.
- Cleared.
- Bounced.
- Cancelled.

Mandatory rules:
1. A recorded or deposited cheque is not collected rent.
2. Pending, bounced and cancelled cheques do not reduce outstanding rent.
3. Clearance creates exactly one linked settled payment.
4. Retrying clearance must not create another payment.
5. A cheque and its resulting payment must never be counted twice.
6. Allocate partial payments to the oldest unpaid dues on the same lease.
7. Reject overpayments in the MVP; do not silently discard excess amounts.
8. Keep deposits separate from rent and rental income.
9. Use BigDecimal and appropriate MySQL decimal columns.
10. Perform financial mutations transactionally and protect against concurrent over-allocation.
11. Preserve historical amounts and audit trails.
12. Correct settled transactions through documented reversals rather than deletion.
13. Apply idempotency to payment recording and external callbacks where relevant.

Distinguish:
- Expected rent within a reporting period.
- Settled collections within that period.
- Outstanding and overdue balances as of a selected date.
- Future unpaid dues, which are not yet overdue.

Do not hard-code VAT solely from a residential/commercial label. Support owner tax status, supply classification, effective dates and explicit tax treatment. Keep exempt and zero-rated treatments distinct. Verify legal claims against current official Omani sources; otherwise mark them for confirmation.
